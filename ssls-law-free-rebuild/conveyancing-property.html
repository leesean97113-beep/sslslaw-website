Overview

Conveyancing & Property in Petaling Jaya, Selangor

Property transactions involve significant sums and strict timelines. We conduct title searches, prepare or review the sale and purchase agreement, coordinate with the other party’s solicitors and the financier, and attend to stamping and registration so that the transfer and charge are properly completed.

We also prepare and review tenancy and sub-tenancy agreements for residential and commercial premises, acting for landlords or tenants.

The Process

How a Matter Usually Proceeds

1. Searches and due diligence

We check the title, any encumbrances and restrictions, and the consents the transaction will need.

2. Sale and purchase agreement

The agreement is negotiated and signed, and the deposit is usually paid on signing.

3. Financing and consents

Where there is a loan, we prepare or review the loan documentation and obtain any required consents.

4. Completion

The balance price is paid, the documents are stamped, the transfer and charge are presented for registration, and vacant possession is handed over.

Every matter is different, and the steps and timing depend on the facts and the other parties involved.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a subsale and buying from a developer?

A subsale is a purchase from an existing owner, on terms negotiated between the parties. New housing bought from a licensed developer is generally sold on the statutory standard form agreement under the Housing Development (Control and Licensing) Act 1966, whose terms are largely fixed.

Who pays the legal fees and stamp duty?

In a typical sale, the purchaser bears the stamp duty on the transfer and on any loan documents, and each party pays its own solicitors, although the parties can agree otherwise. Solicitors’ fees for conveyancing are regulated by the Solicitors’ Remuneration Order.

Does a tenancy agreement need to be stamped?

Yes. A tenancy agreement should be stamped with the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN). An unstamped agreement may generally not be admitted as evidence in court until the duty and any penalty are paid.
