Overview

Civil Litigation in Petaling Jaya, Selangor

Litigation can be costly and time-consuming, so the first step is an honest assessment of the claim or defence, the evidence available and the realistic outcomes. Where a dispute can sensibly be resolved by negotiation or settlement, we will say so.

The Process

How a Matter Usually Proceeds

1. Initial review

We review the documents and facts, identify the legal issues and time limits, and explain your options.

2. Before proceedings

Many disputes begin with a letter of demand or a response to one, and some settle at this stage.

3. Court proceedings

If a claim is filed, the court manages the case through pleadings, case management, documents and witness statements to trial.

4. Judgment and enforcement

After judgment, we advise on appeal options and on enforcing or complying with the court’s orders.

Every matter is different, and the steps and timing depend on the facts and the other parties involved.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to bring a claim?

Time limits apply. Under the Limitation Act 1953, many claims in contract and tort must generally be brought within six years from when the cause of action accrued, but different periods and exceptions apply to some claims. Seek advice early so a claim is not lost because of time.

Which court will hear my case?

It depends mainly on the amount claimed and the type of claim. In general, claims up to RM100,000 are heard in the Magistrates’ Court, claims up to RM1 million in the Sessions Court, and larger or certain other claims in the High Court.

What if the other side does not pay after judgment?

A judgment can be enforced through processes such as seizure and sale of assets, garnishee proceedings against money owed to the debtor, or bankruptcy or winding-up proceedings, depending on the debtor and the circumstances.
