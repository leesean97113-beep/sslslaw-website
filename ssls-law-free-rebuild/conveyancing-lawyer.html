Buying or selling property involves several documents, searches, consents and deadlines. We act for purchasers, vendors and financiers in residential and commercial property transactions, and explain each step in plain terms.

We act in sub-sale (secondary market) purchases and sales, purchases from housing developers, refinancing, and transfers between family members.

How We Can Assist

  • Sale and purchase agreements for sub-sale and developer properties
  • Title searches, bankruptcy and winding-up searches, and property due diligence
  • Loan and financing documents, and perfection of transfer and charge
  • State consent applications for leasehold or restricted titles
  • Transfers by way of love and affection between family members
  • Discharge of charge and redemption of existing loans
  • Stamping of documents and real property gains tax (RPGT) forms

What to Expect

  1. Initial consultation and review of the booking form or letter of offer
  2. Searches on the title and the parties
  3. Preparation and signing of the sale and purchase agreement
  4. Stamping, RPGT submissions and any required consents
  5. Loan documentation with the financier, if any
  6. Completion, payment of the balance purchase price and registration of the transfer

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a sub-sale purchase usually take?

Many sale and purchase agreements give three months from the date of the agreement (or from the date it becomes unconditional) to complete, often with a further one-month extension subject to late payment interest. Where state consent is needed, the timeline may be longer.

What documents should I prepare as a purchaser?

Usually a copy of your identity card, the booking receipt or acceptance letter, and the letter of offer from your bank if you are taking a loan. We will tell you if anything else is needed for your matter.

Who pays the stamp duty on the transfer?

The purchaser usually pays the stamp duty on the memorandum of transfer and on the loan agreement. Exemptions may apply in some cases, for example for eligible first-time home buyers who are Malaysian citizens. The current rates and exemptions should be confirmed for each transaction.

How are conveyancing legal fees calculated?

Legal fees for conveyancing in Peninsular Malaysia are governed by the Solicitors' Remuneration Order 2023. Disbursements such as stamp duty, search and registration fees are separate. We provide a written quotation before we begin.
